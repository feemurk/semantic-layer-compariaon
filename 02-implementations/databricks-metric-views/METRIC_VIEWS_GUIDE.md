## 2. Model structure

One metric view per fact grain, plus a stock-group variant of each WWI grain. Sources are the gold tables of SPEC § 5; joins are many-to-one `LEFT OUTER` lookups with `rely.at_most_one_match: true`, which the platform guarantees (one current row per durable key, unique surrogate keys).

| View | Source | Grain | Joins (alias → table) | Metrics |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `wwi_sales` | `wwi.fact_sale` | invoice line | `inv_cal`, `dlv_cal` → `dim_date` (role-played); `cust`, `bill_cust` → `dim_customer`; `geo` → `dim_city`; `item` → `dim_stock_item` with nested `item_current`; `rep` → `dim_employee` | B-W1–B-W4, W-S1, W-M1 |
| `wwi_sales_by_stock_group` | SQL: `fact_sale` × `bridge_stock_item_group` with `is_first_group` | invoice line × stock group | as `wwi_sales`, plus `grp` → `dim_stock_group` | W-C1, stock-group grains of the sales metrics |
| `wwi_orders` | `wwi.fact_order` | order line | `ord_cal`, `cust`, `geo`, `item` + `item_current`, `rep`, `picker_emp` | B-W5, W-S2 |
| `wwi_orders_by_stock_group` | SQL: `fact_order` × bridge with `is_first_group` | order line × stock group | as `wwi_orders`, plus `grp` | stock-group grains of B-W5 and W-S2 |
| `wwi_stock` | SQL: `fact_stock_holding_daily` LEFT JOIN same-day units sold per item | stock item × day | `item` | W-M2, W-C2 |
| `wwi_stock_by_stock_group` | SQL: the `wwi_stock` source × bridge | stock item × day × stock group | `item`, `grp` | stock-group grains of W-M2 and W-C2 |
| `nyc_trips` | `nyctaxi.fact_trip` | trip | `pickup_cal` → `dim_date`; `pu`, `dz` → `dim_zone` (role-played); `pay` → `dim_payment_type` | B-N1–B-N5, N-S1, N-S2, N-M1, N-M2, N-C1, N-C2 |

Modeling patterns:

- **Role-playing dimensions** are separate join aliases over one table (`inv_cal` / `dlv_cal`, `cust` / `bill_cust`, `pu` / `dz`); each role's fields are prefixed (`pickup_zone`, `dropoff_zone`). NYC dates are taken from the fact's own `pickup_date` and `dropoff_date` columns. Their period fields are `DATE_TRUNC` expressions over the date field, which the window measures require (§ 3.3).
- **SCD2, as-was and as-is.** `item` joins on the surrogate `stock_item_key` (as-was attributes such as `color`); the nested `item_current` joins `item` on the durable `wwi_stock_item_id` and `is_current`, exposing `current_color` (Q-W-S1-D4). A nested attribute is addressed by its path, `item.item_current.color`.
- **Many-to-many stock groups (W-C1, B-02).** A many-to-one join to the bridge would silently pick one group per item. Instead the `*_by_stock_group` views take a SQL source that expands each fact line to one row per group of its item. The source flags the line's first group with `is_first_group`, so deduplicated measures count each line once.
- **Chasm trap (W-C2, B-03).** Stock holdings and sales meet only at (item, day). `wwi_stock` pre-aggregates sales to that grain inside the SQL source and left-joins it to the dense daily snapshot. `units_sold` stays NULL on days without sales, as in the oracle. The view exposes only dimensions both facts share; a territory question (Q-W-C2-D4) returns NULL with a stated reason instead of a fanned-out number.
- **Agent metadata** (`comment`, `display_name`, `synonyms`, `format`) is generated from `01-spec/glossary.yaml`, so every stack reads one vocabulary. The builder drops any synonym that names another member of the same view, and fails on a synonym shared by two members. `display_name` is unique per view.

Conventions enforced by `lint_metric_views.py`:

- lower_snake_case names, and `fields` rather than `dimensions`;
- every qualifier is `source` or a join alias;
- no aggregate in a field, and every measure aggregates;
- `MEASURE()` names a defined measure of the same view, and composition is acyclic;
- window `order` is a field, and coarser window levels are expressions over the finest;
- synonym and `display_name` limits (10 synonyms, 255 characters);
- formats are restricted to `number`, `currency` (with an ISO 4217 code) and `percentage`, each with `decimal_places` of type `exact`.

## 3. Calculation semantics

### 3.1 Additive, distinct and filtered measures

`SUM`/`COUNT` measures aggregate the joined rows that pass the query's `WHERE`; many-to-one joins cannot fan out, so `trip_count = COUNT(1)` is the fact row count at every grain. `invoice_count` and `distinct_od_pairs` are `COUNT(DISTINCT ...)` and are re-evaluated per group. They are never summed from lower grains, so they are exactly non-additive (B-W4, N-M1). Measure-scoped filters use the aggregate `FILTER` clause: `card_tips = SUM(source.tip_amount) FILTER (WHERE source.payment_type = 1)` (N-S2). A measure whose name equals a source column references the column as `source.<column>` to avoid self-reference.

### 3.2 Ratios by composition

Ratios are measures over measures, evaluated from the aggregated numerator and denominator at the query grain (ratio of sums, never a sum of ratios). `gross_margin_pct`, `fare_per_mile`, `stock_group_revenue_share`, `days_of_supply`, `revenue_yoy_pct` and `pace_index` are all compositions. Every ratio casts to `DOUBLE` and guards the denominator with `NULLIF(..., 0)`, matching the oracle's NULL-on-zero rule (SPEC § 9.3).

### 3.3 Window measures

| Measure | Window | Semantics |
|---|---|---|
| `closing_units` (W-M2) | `snapshot_date` `current`, `semiadditive: last` | Sum across items; last snapshot day across time |
| `units_sold_91d` (W-C2) | `snapshot_date` `trailing 91 day inclusive`, `semiadditive: last` | 91 days ending on and including the anchor day |
| `revenue_ytd` (N-C1) | `pickup_date` `cumulative` + `pickup_year` `current` | Sum from 1 January of the anchor's year to the anchor |
| `revenue_stly_ytd` (N-C1) | both specs with `offset: -1 year` | The same frame one year earlier; NULL when the shifted frame has no data (2023 anchors) |
| `trips_mtd`, `trips_ytd` (N-C2) | `cumulative` + `pickup_month` / `pickup_year` `current` | Period-to-date counts |
| `days_elapsed_in_month`, `days_elapsed_in_year` (N-C2) | `pickup_date` `current`, `semiadditive: last` | Day of month / year of the anchor |
| `revenue_all_groups`, `backordered_lines_all_groups` (W-C1, W-S2) | `stock_group` `all` | Deduplicated total with the stock group removed from context (coarser level of detail) |

`range: all` gives the share-of-parent denominator without a second query. `stock_group_revenue_share = revenue / revenue_all_groups` holds the group in the numerator and removes it from the denominator.

### 3.4 Filters and windows

A query `WHERE` on a metric view filters rows before measures are evaluated, so a filter on a window's order field can cut the window: `WHERE pickup_month = DATE '2024-03-01'` leaves `revenue_ytd` only March, and `revenue_stly_ytd` no prior-year rows. Every question that uses a window reaching beyond its anchor (`cumulative`, `trailing`, or an `offset`) therefore groups by the anchor field over the unfiltered view and selects the anchor in outer SQL. `current` and `all` frames are unaffected, so W-M2 questions filter the snapshot date directly. `test_window_queries_filter_outside_the_window` enforces this pattern; the engine's filter-before-window order is verification item V-31.

### 3.5 Window anchor and member coverage (B-07, B-08)

The oracle anchors period-to-date metrics on the **calendar** end of the period (SPEC § 9.1). A metric view window is evaluated per value of its order field, and a coarser group takes the `semiadditive: last` value. The anchor of a (month, member) group is therefore the member's **last date with data** in that month (V-30). The two definitions agree whenever the member has a trip on the period's last day. Exposure on the real-data B0, measured on the oracle's month × member grid of N-C1 and N-C2:

| Member dimension | Grid rows | Member has no trips in the month | Last trip before month end | Members affected |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pickup borough | 192 | 0 | 4 | Staten Island, EWR |
| Payment type | 144 | 23 | 1 | Unknown |
| Vendor | 72 | 4 | 7 | 6 |
| Pickup hour | 576 | 0 | 0 | — |

Rows with no trips in the month are absent from a metric view result, because dimension members are reachable only through fact rows (B-08). The same mechanism makes Q-N-S2-D4 list only the payment codes with 2024 trips, while the oracle lists all seven codes. The question's SQL states this rather than padding the result with literal rows. Q-W-S2-P builds its shared month axis from the order and sale rows, so a month without either fact would be missing. Every 2015 month has both on B0, so the answer matches, but the sparse oracle fixtures show the gap. Reconciliation reports the affected keys as missing (absent rows) or different (early anchor). It does not hide them. The workaround is a calendar-dense, member-dense source (calendar × member cross join left-joined to daily facts). Its cost is a source of `days × members` rows per member dimension, plus one view per dimension. The study records the divergence instead (§ 8).

### 3.6 Time and calendars

NYC period fields are first-of-period dates (`pickup_year = DATE_TRUNC('YEAR', pickup_date)`), so they sort and window correctly. Queries return `YEAR(pickup_year)` where the oracle returns the year number. WWI exposes both calendar (`calendar_year`, `month_start_date`) and fiscal (`fiscal_year`, November start) attributes from `dim_date`; the fiscal calendar is a dimension attribute, not a window level.

## 4. Security model

Two mutually exclusive deployment profiles, because a metric view cannot be materialized when any source has row filters, column masks or ABAC policies (B-01):

| Profile | Bundle target | Security | Materialization | E8 lane |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| governed (default) | `governed` | ABAC row filter policies on `slc.wwi` and `slc.nyctaxi` | None | Yes |
| accelerated | `accelerated` | Policies dropped | `daily_trip_cube`, `daily_sales_cube` | No |

The policies govern the shared gold tables, so the profile applies to every stack on the workspace. Switching profiles is a redeploy of this bundle.

SPEC § 10 implementation (governed):

1. **Functions** ([security/01_row_filter_functions.sql](security/01_row_filter_functions.sql)). `rls_wwi_southeast(city_key)` keeps rows whose as-was city is in the Southeast territory. `rls_nyc_manhattan_pickup(pu_location_id)` keeps Manhattan pickups. `rls_deny_all()` returns no inventory rows, so W-M2 and W-C2 return NULL for the territory role. The lookup tables (`dim_city`, `dim_zone`) carry no policy, because a row filter cannot reference a table that has its own active row filter.
2. **Tags** ([security/02_tags.sql](security/02_tags.sql)). `slc_rls_scope` marks the tables and `slc_rls_key` marks the key columns. Both are governed tags created by an account admin before deployment: a prerequisite outside the bundle.
3. **Policies** ([security/03_policies.sql](security/03_policies.sql)). Schema-level `CREATE OR REPLACE POLICY ... ROW FILTER ... FOR TABLES WHEN has_tag_value(...) MATCH COLUMNS ... USING COLUMNS (...)`, one per role and rule, applied to the account groups `slc_territory_southeast` and `slc_pickup_manhattan`. The dropoff role of NYC is not filtered: the policy matches only `pu_location_id`.
4. **Grants** ([security/04_grants.sql](security/04_grants.sql)). `USE SCHEMA, SELECT` on `slc.semantic` only. A metric view reads its sources with its owner's privileges (V-27), so analysts need no grant on the gold schemas. Whether the ABAC policies on those sources still evaluate the *querying* principal through a metric view is V-28. E8 depends on it, and it is confirmed only on a workspace.

Deploy order matters. The governed profile replaces the views first, removing any materialization, and only then attaches the policies. The accelerated profile drops the policies before creating materialized views. `DROP POLICY` has no `IF EXISTS`, so the deploy job treats `POLICY_NOT_FOUND` as already removed.

## 5. Performance behavior

- **Live path (both profiles).** Queries compile to SQL over the Delta gold tables on serverless SQL. A metric view joins only the tables that the query's selected fields and measures need (joins page). Date filters prune through the platform's liquid clustering (`fact_trip` on `pickup_date`, `fact_sale` on `invoice_date`, `fact_order` on `order_date`, the stock snapshot on `snapshot_date`), and the gold tables declare RELY primary and foreign keys.
- **Materialized path (accelerated).** `daily_trip_cube` holds the additive NYC measures at (pickup date, hour, pickup borough, dropoff borough, payment type, vendor), clustered by `pickup_date`. `daily_sales_cube` holds the additive WWI sales measures at (invoice date, territory, customer category, stock item, salesperson). Both use `schedule: every 1 hour` and `refresh_policy: auto` (incremental when possible, otherwise full), and the deploy job issues one `REFRESH MATERIALIZED VIEW` per view after creation. Distinct counts and window measures are not materialized: distinct counts cannot be rolled up from a cube, and window measures are evaluated at query time. Queries that need them take the live path. Which questions the engine actually serves from a materialization is measured per query in the E3 run, not asserted here.
- **Measurement.** `jobs/reconcile.py` records per query the first (cold) run and the median of three warm runs at concurrency 1 into `slc.ops.mv_reconciliation`, tagged with the profile. Concurrency-10 timings and the governed-vs-accelerated comparison for E3 run in the common load runner (SPEC § 8); the floor to compare against is the raw-SQL baseline in `01-spec/oracle/ORACLE_REPORT.md`.

## 6. Deployment and CI/CD (E5)

`databricks.yml` defines the bundle `slc-metric-views` with targets `governed` (default) and `accelerated`. Its `sync.paths` includes `../../01-spec/oracle`, so the reconcile job reads the oracle's expected answers and comparison code from the same commit. Three serverless jobs (`environment_version: '6'`):

| Job | Tasks |
|---|---|
| `slc_metric_views_deploy` | `deploy.py --profile <p>` → `reconcile.py --profile <p>` (full 92-query run; fails on query errors) |
| `slc_metric_views_reconcile` | `reconcile.py` only |
| `slc_metric_views_gate` | `reconcile.py --select 'Q-*-P' --repeat 1 --fail-on-mismatch` (12 `P` questions; fails on any difference) |

The workflow [s4-metric-views.yml](../../.github/workflows/s4-metric-views.yml) runs on every push touching this stack, the oracle answers or the glossary:

1. `lint`: both builders in `--check` mode, then the test suite.
2. `test` (GitHub environment `test`): `bundle validate`, `deploy`, the deploy job, then the gate.
3. `promote` (environment `prod`, `main` only): the same sequence.

Authentication is workload identity federation (`DATABRICKS_AUTH_TYPE: github-oidc`), with no stored token. Rollback is a redeploy of the previous commit. `databricks/setup-cli@main` follows the official example and is the one unpinned dependency.

| Databricks | E1 reconciliation of all 92 queries, window measures included; E8 per role | Pending a workspace (`STATUS.md`) |

Apache Spark 4.3.0 ships metric views (`CreateMetricView`, `ResolveMetricView`), but only at YAML 0.1. Reproduced locally: `version: 1.1` is rejected as an invalid version, a `window` key is rejected as an unrecognized field, and a composed `MEASURE()` fails with `INTERNAL_ERROR`. The study's definitions therefore cannot execute on open-source Spark. [tools/local_emulator.py](tools/local_emulator.py) checks them by rewriting each view into a relation (source left-joined to every join, fields projected by name) and expanding `MEASURE(m)` to its expression. That is exact for non-window measures and is not evidence of Databricks behavior. The 27 queries that use a window measure (W-M2, W-C1, W-C2, N-C1, N-C2 and their questions, plus Q-W-S2-D4) are resolved but not executed locally.

## 8. Limitations and boundaries

| ID | Boundary | Consequence in S4 | Workaround and cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| B-01 | No materialization over sources with RLS, masks or ABAC | Governed and accelerated profiles cannot coexist on one workspace | Two profiles; a materialized copy would need an unsecured duplicate of the gold tables |
| B-02 | Many-to-one join over many-to-many data picks the first match | Bridge cannot be a join | SQL-source expansion plus `is_first_group` and `range: all`; two extra views |
| B-03 | No chasm-safe multi-fact join without `one_to_many` or Beta relationships | W-C2 needs a pre-joined source | Second fact pre-aggregated to the shared grain inside the SQL source; non-shared dimensions are unavailable (Q-W-C2-D4 returns NULL) |
| B-07 | Window anchor is the last date with data in the group, not the calendar period end | N-C1 and N-C2 month × member values differ for sparse members (§ 3.5) | Calendar × member dense source; cost `days × members` rows per dimension |
| B-08 | Members are reachable only through fact rows | Members without facts in scope are absent (Q-N-S2-D4, empty month × member cells) | Dimension-driven outer query per question; not expressible in the metric view itself |

Further limits:

- **Relationships between metric views (Beta).** These were considered for W-C2 and W-S2 and not used. They are Beta, so outside SPEC § 3 scope for production patterns, and the SQL-source approach answers every question with GA features.
- **Restricted reconciliation.** The oracle does not yet carry restricted expected answers per role (SPEC § 10), so E8 cannot be scored until they are produced.
- **Prerequisites outside the bundle:** the account-level governed tags `slc_rls_scope` and `slc_rls_key` with their allowed values; the account groups `slc_territory_southeast` and `slc_pickup_manhattan`; and the platform bundle's B0 build in catalog `slc`. The catalog name `slc` is fixed in the generated definitions.
- **Window measures and query filters.** Self-service clients that push a date filter into the `WHERE` clause of a window-measure query get truncated windows (§ 3.4). The E2 and E7 lanes measure how Genie and the harness handle this.
